# PERMANENT RULE — OWNER COMMUNICATION AND APPROVALS (owner, 2026-11-25)

Applies to Muse, Claude (interactive, PC2 worker, cloud routines) and every future agent, on every project.

Whenever an agent needs the owner's approval, permission, input or a business decision, explain it in simple,
everyday English, as if to someone with no technical knowledge. No logs, code, long reports or jargon.

Every approval request includes:
1. **THE PROBLEM**: what is wrong or what needs to happen.
2. **WHY IT MATTERS**: how it affects the owner, the business or customers.
3. **THE OPTIONS**: the choices.
4. **RECOMMENDATION**: which option and why.
5. **COST**: money? one-time or recurring?
6. **RISK**: what could go wrong; can it be undone?
7. **THE DECISION**: one clear, direct question (answerable: Yes / No / Explain more).

Several approvals: number each question separately.

Standing rules:
- Short and easy to understand; translate technical issues into business language.
- Never hide important risks, costs or consequences.
- Don't ask the owner to do work the agents are authorized and able to do.
- Don't re-ask for something already authorized.
- Muse and Claude resolve technical disagreements together before involving the owner.
- Keep working on other authorized work while waiting. Silence is not approval.
- Technical evidence goes in the engineering notes, not in the approval request.

Example:
> **Approval needed: Website security**
> Problem: Someone could reach our server without going through the website's normal protection.
> Why it matters: The site is more exposed to attacks.
> Recommendation: Close the unnecessary access point.
> Cost: $0. Risk: Low, once we confirm the other websites still work; it can be undone.
> Question: Do you approve closing this access point after we confirm your other websites keep working?
> You can answer: Yes, No, or Explain more.
